# Shared visual system

These rules apply to every mode unless its dedicated contract narrows them.

## Identity

The result is a source-responsive impressionist transformation, not a preset filter. The source provides facts and relationships; art direction decides which facts remain photographic, which are translated into paint, and which are simplified or omitted.

## Decision priority

1. Preserve the semantic anchor and required identity.
2. Obey the selected mode's source-pixel policy.
3. Keep decisive spatial relationships readable.
4. Choose Monet zones from source evidence rather than painterly clichés.
5. Let light determine palette temperature, brush behavior, and edge loss.
6. Give each surface and depth layer an appropriate brush family.
7. Make non-Monet regions defer in contrast, chroma, detail, and texture.
8. Preserve selectivity; remove unplanned painting before adding more texture.
9. When `sparse_social` is selected, protect intentional quiet fields as firmly as semantic anchors.

## Impressionability Map

Score candidate zones from 0–4 on five dimensions:

- **Light** — visible illumination, backlight, dapple, glow, or color-bearing shadow.
- **Atmosphere** — fog, haze, smoke, rain, air perspective, or soft distance.
- **Reflection** — water, glass, wet pavement, metal, or reflected light.
- **Color variation** — local warm/cool shifts, flowers, foliage, sky, illuminated surfaces, or mixed reflections.
- **Edge instability** — motion, distance, reflection, mist, glare, or naturally dissolving boundaries.

The primary Monet zone normally has the highest total score. A lower-scoring area may win only when it carries the user's explicit subject or meaning; record that override in the card.

## Transformation strengths

- **M1 — Hint:** light-temperature shifts, limited color decomposition, gentle softening; strokes remain subtle.
- **M2 — Partial:** visible broken color, selective directional strokes, and a controlled set of broken or lost edges.
- **M3 — Strong:** complete brush construction, chromatic darks, optical warm/cool vibration, and atmosphere-led form.
- **M4 — Abstract:** broad or sweeping surface rhythm, greater subject dissolution, nonliteral color, and reduced spatial certainty while retaining the semantic nucleus.

Strength changes the amount of transformation, not the selected mode's source-pixel contract.

## Composition

- Preserve source orientation unless the user requests a target ratio.
- Maintain one semantic anchor and one dominant Monet zone.
- Use secondary Monet zones only when they support the same light, reflection, or color event.
- Let the eye move between factual anchor and painted event through a source-derived axis, gaze, path, reflection, or light direction.
- Avoid automatic central framing and mechanical half-and-half splits.

## Density profiles

### Immersive

Use the selected mode's natural field coverage. Even a fully painted frame still needs contrast between active strokes and calmer passages.

### Sparse Social

Reduce the scene to one dominant active cluster and at most two supporting marks. Prefer a single source-derived graphic grammar: silhouette, contour, field, rhythm, or cutout. A second grammar may support it but must not compete.

Exception: Zine Hybrid with `cutout_isolation` permits exactly two active source-derived pieces—the photographic anchor and painted carrier—and no supporting marks or second grammar. The anchor is visually primary or balanced with the carrier; 30–60% of the frame is unmarked paper.

Translate source material through five actions:

- retain one or two defining forms or relationships;
- merge repeated or noisy elements into a larger mass;
- omit nonessential descriptive detail;
- transform retained evidence into the selected graphic grammar;
- expose blank, paper-like, flat-color, haze, water, wall, or sky fields as negative space.

For `sparse_social`, target 60–90% removal of descriptive detail, 45–85% quiet or low-information area, and 10–35% active graphic area. These are visual budgets, not pixel measurements. Preserve one thumbnail anchor and one decisive color or light event at reduced size.

## Background treatments

Choose one treatment for non-Monet regions:

- **Preserve:** keep source pixels truthful.
- **Simplify:** reduce detail, contrast, saturation, and texture.
- **Line:** use restrained graphite or ink contours with low contrast and incomplete edges.
- **Paper:** remove content into a quiet warm or neutral field with minimal pigment trace.

The background must remain subordinate. Do not combine high-contrast line art, heavy paper texture, and strong paint in the same supporting field.

In `sparse_social`, a quiet field may be retained photography, simplified color, paper, haze, water, wall, or a fully painted low-information passage according to the mode. Do not violate a no-photo mode by using exposed source pixels as negative space.

## Transition strategies

- **Brush intrusion:** source-derived strokes cross into or out of the photographic boundary.
- **Dissolve:** photographic detail gradually becomes broken color and lost edge.
- **Light bridge:** transformation follows the actual footprint of illumination.
- **Reflection bridge:** painted reflection answers a photographic subject across a real axis.
- **Torn field:** a restrained fibrous paper boundary; Zine Hybrid only.

For `cutout_isolation`, Torn Field is the single organizing boundary system rather than a decorative overlay. It must follow both selected source-region contours and must not create extra scraps.

For a photographic human, animal, product, landmark, or identity-sensitive object, treat the anchor as an original-pixel composite layer. The painted carrier may be generated, but it must not regenerate, restyle, or cover the anchor.

Transitions must follow source geometry or light. Never use a generic gradient or decorative paint splash.

## Typography

No text by default. When text is requested:

- use one exact title of two to four words or one very short caption;
- keep it subordinate to the image;
- do not invent dates, locations, quotations, credits, coordinates, barcodes, or magazine filler;
- use deterministic composition when spelling is production-critical.

## Material

Painted zones may show matte oil-paint or printed-paint character, but the image should not become a photographed canvas mockup. Keep lighting consistent with the source. Avoid frame shadows, gallery walls, curled paper, decorative easels, or exaggerated impasto across every surface.
