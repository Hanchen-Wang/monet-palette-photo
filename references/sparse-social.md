# Sparse Social presentation profile

Use this cross-mode profile when the result should read quickly on social media, feel less visually saturated, or derive a zine-like graphic system from the photograph. It changes density and abstraction, not the selected mode's source-pixel contract.

## Identity

The result should feel edited rather than filled: one semantic anchor, one decisive Monet color or light event, one primary graphic grammar, and deliberate quiet space. Sparse does not mean generic minimalism; every retained shape and color relation must be traceable to the source.

## Shape Extraction Card

Resolve the `shape_abstraction` object in the Monet Transformation Card:

- `semantic_minimum`: the smallest source relationship that still carries the scene's meaning;
- `dominant_gesture`: the main directional force, such as a gaze, horizon, path, reflection axis, cloud band, shoreline, or figure lean;
- `source_shape_candidates`: one or two source forms that can survive strong reduction;
- `retain`: one or two defining forms or relationships;
- `merge`: repeated or noisy elements that can become one mass, band, rhythm, or field;
- `omit`: descriptive details that do not support identity, place, light, or gesture;
- `transform`: source evidence translated into the selected graphic grammar;
- `expose`: source-derived areas allowed to become blank, paper-like, flat, hazy, watery, or otherwise low-information;
- `primary_grammar`: exactly one of `silhouette`, `contour`, `field`, `rhythm`, or `cutout`;
- `supporting_grammar`: zero or one different grammar;
- `detail_removal_percent`, `quiet_area_percent`, and `active_shape_percent`: visual budgets rather than pixel measurements;
- `thumbnail_anchor`: the single form or color event that must remain recognizable at roughly one-quarter display size.

If any of the semantic minimum, source-shape candidates, or source-pixel boundary is genuinely ambiguous, ask one concise question. Infer the numeric budgets from the source and profile; do not ask the user to choose percentages.

## Graphic grammars

- `silhouette`: use a recognizable mass with selective internal gaps.
- `contour`: use a few incomplete directional lines; never trace every edge.
- `field`: organize the image through one or two large source-derived color or paper areas.
- `rhythm`: compress repetitions into a paced sequence of marks, bands, or intervals.
- `cutout`: use flat source-derived shapes and restrained torn or cut boundaries.

Use one primary grammar and at most one supporting grammar. Monet brushwork remains the color-and-light event; graphic devices organize it rather than compete with it.

## Density budget

- Remove 60–90% of descriptive detail.
- Keep 45–85% of the artwork quiet or low-information.
- Keep the active graphic cluster near 10–35% of the artwork.
- Use one dominant mass and no more than two supporting marks or clusters.
- Preserve internal negative space inside large shapes where the source supports it.
- Place breathing room in front of a gaze, path, wave, light direction, or dominant diagonal when possible.

Quiet areas may still contain subtle paper grain, flat color, haze, calm water, sky, wall, or very low-density paint. They must not accumulate decorative scraps, filler type, scattered dots, or all-over brush texture.

## Mode interaction

- Full Impression: every visible region remains painted; quiet areas use broad low-information color fields rather than source pixels.
- Subject Monetization: let the subject or its silhouette form the active cluster and reduce context to one or two cues.
- Atmospheric Monetization: retain truthful anchor pixels while allowing large sky, water, haze, or light fields to become visually quiet.
- Zine Hybrid: a photographic anchor, one paint event, and at most one paper or line support. Torn edges may be prominent but should remain one coherent boundary system.
- Distilled Monet: no source pixels; express the semantic minimum through broad fields, rhythm, silhouette, or cutout-like painted shapes.

## Prompt implications

State the semantic minimum, primary grammar, dominant active cluster, areas to merge or omit, quiet-field locations, thumbnail anchor, and approximate density budgets. Do not copy analytical field labels or percentages when the same intent can be expressed visibly and naturally.

## Hard avoids

- equal detail across the whole frame;
- several independent collage clusters;
- literal tracing of all source contours;
- dense scrapbook layering, stickers, tape, fake print metadata, or decorative captions;
- arbitrary shapes or accent colors not supported by the source;
- confusing blank space with missing composition;
- violating the selected mode's source-pixel policy to create emptiness.

## Profile QA

- [ ] The semantic anchor is legible at reduced size.
- [ ] One grammar dominates and any second grammar clearly supports it.
- [ ] One active cluster carries the visual event.
- [ ] Quiet fields occupy at least as much attention as active marks.
- [ ] Retained forms, palette, and gesture remain traceable to the source.
- [ ] Removed detail has not erased identity or the decisive spatial relation.
- [ ] No filler marks, metadata, or unrelated motifs were added.
